Projects

My PhD is on translation: how the sequence of an mRNA sets how much protein a cell makes from it. The first three projects below make up that work. The others are collaborations, group projects and tools I build alongside it.

seq2te Active

seq2te predicts a gene’s translation efficiency from its mRNA sequence, in a named cell type or condition, and shows which parts of the sequence carry the signal. It is the methods and benchmark paper of my PhD, and it grew out of my earlier proTE work.

The model combines information measured at different resolutions: ribosome footprint coverage along the transcript, translation efficiency per gene and per isoform, RNA structure, RNA-binding protein motifs and features of the encoded protein. The question is whether this combination beats any single sequence foundation model. Transfer to cell types and conditions the model has not seen is a main result, because a prediction is only useful in the tissue where the transcript is read. We also interpret the model, by tying predictions to known sequence features and by looking for transcripts that sit apart in its learned representation.

genTE Active

genTE takes on the design question that follows. Given a protein, which mRNA will translate it with a chosen profile in a chosen cell state? The protein stays fixed and only the message changes.

Designs are judged on how well the model ranks different mRNAs for the same protein, since that is the choice a designer faces. Every evaluation carries controls that separate translation from mRNA abundance and from transcript length, and each design is scored by a model from a different family than the one that proposed it. The aim is constructs good enough that a wet lab will test them.

TE labels Active

Translation efficiency labels built from public studies with matched Ribo-seq and RNA-seq. They are built at every resolution the data supports: per gene, per transcript isoform where isoforms can be told apart, and as changes between conditions. Each level passes its own quality checks before use. These labels are the training and evaluation targets for seq2te and genTE.

riboseq-agent Contributor

Owen Rackham’s project, which finds, annotates and processes public ribosome profiling studies, from a research question through to quality-checked footprint tracks. I contribute to the Nextflow processing and to turning aligned reads into coverage tracks for the genome browser.

kitsune Group project

A UoSDDNB group project looking for unannotated small open reading frames, which may encode microproteins, by fine-tuning nucleotide sequence models to predict Ribo-seq and RNA-seq coverage.

locus Group project

UoSDDNB group work. locus finds public omics datasets from a plain-English question, then reads each dataset’s own sample records to count cases and controls and check whether it fits. For bulk RNA-seq studies it runs a first differential expression analysis on a laptop.

Domain experts Active

A framework that distils a collection of scientific papers into a panel of AI assistants, one per field. Each answers only from its own sources and cites a passage for every claim, and its knowledge is plain text that can be read and corrected. I use them to test ideas against the literature in translation, RNA biology, statistics and machine learning.

Playtime On hold

Small browser games that teach biology through play, such as an RNA chain game and a neutrophil chase. Paused for a few months.
